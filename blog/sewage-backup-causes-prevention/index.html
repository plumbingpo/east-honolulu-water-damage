Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in East Honolulu, HI

Home / Blog / Why Sewage Backups Happen in East Honolulu, HI

A sewage backup is one of the most unpleasant emergencies a homeowner can face. Raw sewage entering your living spaces creates immediate health hazards, causes extensive property damage, and requires professional cleanup that can cost thousands of dollars. Understanding why sewage backups occur and taking preventive measures can significantly reduce your risk. East Honolulu Restoration wrote this guide to walk East Honolulu, HI owners through the usual causes and some practical ways to keep a property protected.

Common Causes of Sewage Backups

When something keeps wastewater from draining to the city sewer or your septic tank as designed, it comes back into the building. Usual culprits are tree roots growing into sewer pipes; lines clogged by a buildup of hair, grease, and debris; pipes that are old or have collapsed; combined sewer overflow events; an overloaded municipal system in heavy rain; and toilets used to flush the wrong things, such as feminine products, wipes, and too much paper.

How Tree Roots Block Sewer Pipes

In East Honolulu, many blocked sewer lines turn out to be the work of tree roots. A sewer pipe is a steady supply of moisture, which is exactly what roots go looking for. They work their way in at cracks, joints, and connection points, then slowly fill the inside of the pipe until it blocks and backs up. Older sewer lines made of clay tile are especially easy for roots to invade. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2 to 3 years so root trouble is found ahead of any backup.

How to Prevent a Sewage Backup

Several habits and upgrades help keep sewage out of an East Honolulu home, and the first is a backwater valve on the sewer connection, which stops backflow from the municipal system. Never pour grease or cooking oil down drains. Do not flush feminine products, wipes, or anything other than toilet paper. Have the sewer line inspected and cleaned on a regular schedule. Address slow drains promptly before they become complete blockages. Know the location of your sewer cleanout for emergency access. Think about adding a sewage backup endorsement to your homeowner's policy, or buying sewer line insurance.

Sewage Came Up? Do This First

Cleaning up a sewage backup on your own is something you should never try. Serious illness can come from the dangerous pathogens in sewage, among them parasites, bacteria, and viruses.
