Crawl Space Water Removal: What East Honolulu Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • A look through the hatch shows pooled water or soupy soil
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Contaminated Water

Do not assume the water below your floor is clean. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Termites and Other Pests

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

Air From Below

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

What We Do

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.
